# Georgia appellate costs — O.C.G.A. §§ 5-6-4 to 5-6-6

## Amount of the bill of costs

Section 5-6-4(a) sets the bill of costs for every application to the Supreme
Court for a writ of certiorari. It also sets the costs for applications for
appeals filed in the Supreme Court or the Court of Appeals, and for appeals to
either court. It is not clear whether *every* reaches all three categories or
only the first.

The cost is $80.00 in criminal cases. The cost is also $80.00 in habeas corpus
cases for a person whose liberty is restrained under a sentence imposed by a
state court. The cost is $300.00 in all other civil cases.

## Who pays, and when

Counsel for the applicant or appellant must pay the costs when the application
is filed. For a direct appeal, counsel must pay when the original brief of the
appellant is filed.

Where the writ of certiorari or an application for appeal is granted, there are
no additional costs.

## When costs are not required

Under section 5-6-4(b), costs are not required when any one of the following is
true at the time the costs are due.

- The pro se applicant or pro se appellant is incarcerated at the time of the
  filing.
- Counsel for the applicant or appellant was appointed to represent the
  defendant by the trial court because of the defendant's indigency.
- The applicant, appellant, or counsel for the applicant or appellant files an
  affidavit of indigency.

The statute does not state who is released from the requirement to pay.

## The clerk's prohibition

Under section 5-6-4(c), the clerk must not receive the application for appeal or
the appellant's brief unless the costs have been paid or subsection (b) is
satisfied.

## Costs on reversal

Under section 5-6-5, if there is a judgment of reversal, the appellant is
entitled to a judgment for the amount of the costs in the appellate court
against the appellee. That entitlement arises as soon as the remittitur is
returned to the court below.

## Damages for a frivolous appeal

Under section 5-6-6, the appellate court may award 10 percent damages upon any
judgment for a sum certain which has been affirmed. The court may do so only
when, in the opinion of the court, the case was taken up for delay only.

The award must be entered in the remittitur. The statute does not state who
must enter the award.
