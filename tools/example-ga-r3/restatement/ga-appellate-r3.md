# Georgia appellate practice — plain-language summary

Register R3. Source: O.C.G.A. §§ 5-6-3, 5-6-5, 5-6-6, 5-6-7.

## Words this summary keeps

The appellant is the party who appealed. The appellee is the party on the other
side. A remittitur is the appellate court's order sending a case back to the
lower court. A sum certain is a fixed amount of money. Ore tenus means by
spoken word, not in writing. Want of prosecution means a case is dropped
because a party did not move it forward.

## Briefs when a term runs out

Under section 5-6-3(a), the court may order counsel to file briefs by a set day
before the next term begins. The court may do this whenever it may be unable to decide all cases on its
docket for any term. This applies before the time fixed by law for the next
term to begin.

A case in which briefs are filed under that order is considered heard at the
term to which it was returned. The court determines the case and announces its
decision as soon after the briefs are filed as is practicable.

If the court wants argument beyond the briefs, it may order counsel to give
more argument. Counsel may give it in a brief or in open court.

Under section 5-6-3(b), if counsel for the appellant does not comply with the
order, the case must be dismissed for want of prosecution. That applies where no
sufficient excuse for noncompliance is shown. The statute does not state who
must dismiss the case. This consequence is mandatory.

## Costs when a case is reversed

Section 5-6-5 covers costs after a reversal. If there is a judgment of
reversal, the appellant is entitled to a judgment for the amount of the costs
against the appellee. This happens as soon as the remittitur is returned to the
court below.

## Damages for an appeal taken only to delay

Under section 5-6-6, the appellate court may award 10 percent damages on any
judgment for a sum certain which has been affirmed. The court may do this only when, in
the opinion of the court, the case was taken up for delay only.

The award must be entered in the remittitur. The statute does not state who
must enter it.

## Spoken decisions, and what gets published

Under section 5-6-7, a decision must not be rendered ore tenus. The statute
does not state who is forbidden to render one.

The reporter must publish all judgments of the Supreme Court and the Court of
Appeals in the official reports. The reporter must publish only those opinions
that the courts direct to be published.
