# MLP-1 — Controlled Legal Writing

A controlled natural language for legal text: court rules, statutes,
regulations, and contracts. Published by Michigan Legal Publishing Ltd.

**[Read the standard (PDF)](dist/MLP-1.pdf)** · [ODT](dist/MLP-1.odt) · [Markdown](standard/MLP-1.md)

## What it is

Controlled languages for technical documentation assume a writer may swap any
word for a simpler one. That assumption fails in law. A legal term of art is
not a hard word chosen over an easy one — it is a name for a rule, and
replacing it deletes the rule.

MLP-1 sets out 79 rules and 11 general recommendations for two jobs: drafting
operative text, and restating authoritative text faithfully at a stated
register. Where the writer restates rather than authors, the measure is not
whether the text reads well but whether it says the same thing.

Three things distinguish it from controlled languages built for technical
writing:

- **Terms of art are protected, not simplified.** Paraphrasing a legal
  standard is a violation, not a clarification.
- **Ambiguity in the source is preserved and marked, not resolved.** Removing
  it substitutes the writer's reading for the law.
- **Fidelity is verified, not asserted.** Part 3 sets out a proposition
  ledger, bidirectional mapping, mechanical checks, and version binding.
