# Prohibited wording

Read this before returning any output. The checks are mechanical. Run them against your own draft, not against the source.

**One exception governs the whole file: quoted text.** Rule 2.7 preserves a quotation exactly, including its archaisms, its semicolons, its Latin, and its missing serial commas. Never modernize a quotation. Where a source's own wording is a problem, say so instead of fixing it.

---

## 1. Archaic pointing words

These survive by inheritance. The *here-*, *there-* and *where-* compounds are a common source of ambiguity, because the reader has to guess what they point at.

Do not write: *aforesaid, aforementioned, hereafter* (meaning "in this document"), *hereby, herein, hereinabove, hereinafter, hereinbefore, hereof, hereto, heretofore, hereunder, herewith, said* (as a pointing adjective), *same* (as a pronoun), *thence, thenceforth, thereafter* (meaning "in that document"), *thereby, therefor, therefrom, therein, thereof, thereon, thereto, theretofore, thereunder, therewith, to wit, whatsoever, whensoever, whereas* (in recitals), *whereby, wherefore, wherein, whereof, whereupon, witnesseth, wheresoever.*

Replace the compound with the thing it points at. **If you cannot identify that thing, the source was ambiguous — flag it under Rule 10.4 rather than guessing.**

> Not CLW: Said motion shall be filed within the time period specified hereinabove.
> CLW: The party must file the motion within the period stated in section 4(a).

*Such* as a demonstrative is the same failure: write *the*, *that*, or the thing's name.

---

## 2. Latin that is merely Latin

A Latin **term of art** is preserved (see `preserved-terms.md`). Latin with an exact English equivalent is not.

| Not CLW | CLW |
|---|---|
| inter alia | among others |
| e.g. | for example |
| i.e. | that is |
| etc. | and so on, or rewrite |
| viz. | namely |
| supra, infra | a full citation |
| arguendo | for the sake of argument |
| ab initio | from the beginning |
| pro tanto | to that extent |
| seriatim | one after another |
| *sic* | preserve — it is a signal, not a word |

Never write *etc.* in operative text. A list there is either closed, or opened by a word that opens it (*including*, *such as*). *Etc.* leaves the class for a court to construe.

## 3. Prohibited legalese

| Not CLW | CLW |
|---|---|
| commence | begin, or file |
| deem | restate — say what is treated as what |
| effectuate | perform, carry out |
| endeavor | try |
| evidence (verb) | show |
| forthwith | restate with a stated period, or preserve and flag |
| furnish | give |
| indicate | state, show |
| initiate | begin, file |
| in the event that | if |
| obtain | get, or restate |
| prior to | before |
| pursuant to | under |
| set forth | state, list |
| subsequent to | after |
| terminate | end |
| upon | on, after, when |
| utilize | use |
| whereas | delete, or state the fact |
| with respect to | about, for, under |

*Transmit* is not on this list in a restatement. Preserve it where the source uses it (Rule 2.3).

## 4. Nominalizations

A noun made from a verb hides the action and drags a weak verb and a passive behind it.

| Not CLW | CLW |
|---|---|
| give consideration to | consider |
| make a determination | decide, determine |
| make application | apply |
| make payment | pay |
| conduct an examination | examine |
| effect a transfer | transfer |
| provide notification | notify |
| is in violation of | violates |
| is in compliance with | complies with |
| take into consideration | consider |
| have knowledge of | know |
| render a decision | decide |

**Exception.** Some nominalizations are themselves terms of art: *service, joinder, removal, remand, discovery, consideration* (in contract), *conversion, waiver, forfeiture, preclusion*. The test is whether the noun names a legal concept or merely restates a verb.

## 5. Phrasal verbs

| Not CLW | CLW |
|---|---|
| set forth | state |
| set out | state, list |
| carry out | perform, do |
| put forward | propose, offer |
| bring about | cause |
| lay down | state, require |
| take up | consider, hear |
| hand down | issue |
| turn on | depend on |
| do away with | repeal, end |
| come into force | take effect |

**Exception.** Some are terms of art: *hand down* (an opinion), *hold over* (a tenancy), *set aside* (a judgment), *strike down* (a statute). Preserve these where the source uses them.

---

## 6. Doublets and triplets

Do not write new ones: *null and void, cease and desist, free and clear, each and every, full and complete, give, devise, and bequeath*.

Two exceptions, and both matter:

1. **Settled terms of art.** *Aid and abet, pain and suffering, cruel and unusual, full faith and credit, last will and testament.* Do not split these.
2. **Restatement.** Where the source uses a doublet, keep it. A court may have given each word separate work to do, and dropping one drops content.

---

## 7. Intensifiers and empty hedges

Do not write: *clearly, obviously, plainly, undoubtedly, certainly, of course, needless to say, it goes without saying, very, extremely, quite, rather, somewhat, arguably, in a sense, essentially, basically, generally speaking, as such.*

**Do not confuse an intensifier with a legal standard.** *Clear and convincing, clearly erroneous, plain error, manifest injustice, reasonable, material, substantial,* and *willful* have defined content. They are preserved terms. Never soften, strengthen, or paraphrase one.

> Not CLW: The appellate court will reverse only if the finding was very wrong.
> CLW: The appellate court will reverse only if the finding was clearly erroneous.

---

## 8. Punctuation

- **Serial comma, always.** Its absence creates a real ambiguity about whether the last two items are one item or two. A wage case has turned on exactly this.
- **No semicolon in text you draft.** Two exceptions: quoted text, and a restatement reproducing a source's enumerated series, where the semicolon carries the *; and* / *; or* connector.
- **No `and/or`.** No court has settled what it means.

| You mean | Write |
|---|---|
| A, B, or both | *A or B, or both* |
| A and B together | *A and B* |
| Exactly one | *either A or B, but not both* |
| Any one or more | *one or more of the following* |
| Every item | *each of the following* |

Where the **source** uses `and/or`, preserve it and flag it as ambiguous. Do not resolve it.

---

## 9. Numbers and periods

- Numerals, once: `14 days`, never `fourteen (14) days`.
- Every period states its length, its trigger, and its direction: `within 21 days after service`.

---

## 10. Markers of machine prose

These are worth removing for three reasons: they pad, they smuggle in interpretation, and they make text recognizable as unedited machine output.

Do not write:

- A **sentence-initial connective** in operative text.
- An **opening that announces the text** ("This rule addresses…").
- A **closing synthesis.** It characterizes, and characterization is construction.
- An **"It's not just X, it's Y"** construction. Always a characterization, usually an interpretation.
- A **tricolon** where the source has two items, or one. Match the source's structure.
- An **em dash appending an afterthought.** Use a period.
- A **hedged universal:** *generally, typically, usually, in most cases, often.* These disguise an unstated ambiguity as a tendency.
- An **evaluative adjective:** *important, critical, key, crucial, significant, robust, comprehensive.*
- A **rhetorical question.**
- A **bulleted list where the source has flowing text.**

The last is the most consequential. Converting a source's prose into four bullets tells the reader there are exactly four things. The source may not say that.

---

## Final pass

Run these in order against your own output:

1. No word from sections 1 through 7 outside a quotation.
2. Serial comma in every list of three or more.
3. No semicolon outside a quotation or a reproduced series.
4. No `and/or` outside a quotation.
5. Numerals once. Every period has length, trigger, and direction.
6. Nothing from section 10.
7. Same word for the same thing, every time. Varied vocabulary is evidence of varied meaning.
8. Every sentence within the register's cap, counting each citation, quotation, case name, date, defined term, and parenthetical as one word.

Then confirm the five refusals held. If you cannot confirm one, say so in the output rather than returning silently.
