# Drafting new operative text

Use this when you are writing text that will have legal effect and no one has written it yet: a court rule, a statute, a regulation, an ordinance, an order, or a contract clause.

This is the opposite posture from `restate.md`. There, a source was fixed and your job was fidelity. Here **you are deciding what the law is.** Ambiguity you leave is ambiguity you created.

---

## Step 0 — Confirm you may draft this

Two checks before writing.

**Does a drafting manual govern?** Most state legislatures still teach *shall* as the mandatory operator. A drafter in one of those offices is required to follow the manual. Where a drafting manual, court rule, or agency directive governs, **that authority controls and displaces Rule 3.2.** Check the order of precedence first.

**Does the delegation authorize what you are drafting?** An agency rule that exceeds its enabling statute is void however well it is written. Confirm the grant of authority reaches the subject, the persons bound, and the consequence you intend to impose.

Record the register (R1 for operative text, in almost every case).

---

## Step 1 — Decide the operator before the sentence

The operator is the load-bearing word. Decide what the provision *does* before you decide how it reads.

| You intend | Write |
|---|---|
| A duty | must |
| A prohibition | must not |
| Discretion | may |
| To withhold power | may not |
| A right another must satisfy | is entitled to |
| To negate a duty | need not |
| An automatic consequence | will |

**Never write *shall*.** Every use resolves to one of the seven, or deletes:

| *Shall* is doing this | Write |
|---|---|
| Imposing a duty | must |
| Imposing a prohibition | must not |
| Granting permission | may |
| Withholding authority | may not |
| Stating a future consequence | will, or simple present |
| Nothing at all | delete |

> ✗ The petition shall contain a statement of the issues. The court shall have jurisdiction.
> ✓ The petition must contain a statement of the issues. The court has jurisdiction.

**Never write *should*.** It has no settled deontic force, and it is unavailable at every register — explanatory text included.

See `operators.md` for the negation table. Writing *must not* where you mean *need not* inverts the provision.

---

## Step 2 — Name the actor

A duty needs a bearer. Operative text that imposes a duty without naming who owes it is defective.

> ✗ The notice must be filed within 14 days.
> ✓ The appellant must file the notice within 14 days.

**Do not attach a duty to an inanimate thing.**

> ✗ The motion must include a certificate of service.
> ✓ A party who files a motion must include a certificate of service.

The first describes a property of a document. The second imposes a duty on a person, which is what a rule does.

---

## Step 3 — Write in the singular

Write *a party*, not *parties*. The plural always raises a question the singular does not: does the requirement fall on each member or on the class as a whole?

> ✗ Parties must file responses within 14 days.
> ✓ Each party must file a response within 14 days.

Where a provision genuinely applies to a group acting together, say so:

> ✓ The parties must file a joint status report within 14 days.

Use *each* where you mean the members individually. Use *a* or *an* for the general case.

---

## Step 4 — Order the provision

The reader who needs only the general rule should be able to stop after the first sentence.

1. The general rule.
2. Any exception, limitation, or qualification.
3. The consequence of not complying.
4. Any cross-reference.

> ✓ A party must file the notice of appeal within 30 days after the judgment is entered. A party in a case in which the United States is a party must file within 60 days. If a party does not file within the applicable period, the court of appeals has no jurisdiction over the appeal. Rule 4(a)(5) states when the district court may extend the period.

Put every condition before the duty it governs, and state every condition on which a duty or power depends. A power that depends on a finding says so.

---

## Step 5 — State the consequence, and name its severity

A requirement with no stated consequence is the fact pattern that produces a directory reading. If you want the requirement enforced, say what failure costs.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Jurisdictional** | Non-compliance deprives the court of power. Cannot be cured, waived, or forgiven. |
| **Mandatory** | Non-compliance causes a stated adverse result, but the court may have power to excuse it. |
| **Discretionary** | Non-compliance permits, but does not require, an adverse result. |
| **Administrative** | Non-compliance causes procedural inconvenience only. It does not affect the merits. |

> **Jurisdictional:** A party must file the notice of appeal within 30 days after the judgment is entered. This deadline is jurisdictional. If a party misses it, the court of appeals has no power to hear the appeal, and no court may extend the period.

> **Discretionary:** A party must include a table of authorities. If a party does not, the court may strike the brief.

**Do not label a deadline jurisdictional** unless controlling authority says so, or the consequence is unambiguously jurisdictional. Whether a deadline is jurisdictional is heavily litigated, and getting it wrong is a serious failure.

Put the consequence next to the requirement, not twelve subsections away. A reader who finds the deadline and not the consequence has half the rule.

---

## Step 6 — Remove the ambiguity you created

This is where drafting and restating diverge completely. In a restatement you preserve ambiguity. **Here you eliminate it**, because you are the one who would be leaving it behind.

**Modifier reach.** Where a modifier follows a series, English does not settle whether it reaches the whole series or only the last item, and the competing canons do not settle it either.

> ✗ a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent licensed in this state
> ✓ a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent, if the person is licensed in this state
> ✓ a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent who is licensed in this state. The licensing requirement applies only to an agent.

Use a vertical list, repeat the modifier, or state its reach in a separate sentence.

**Leading modifiers have the same problem.**

> ✗ written notice or demand
> ✓ written notice, or a written demand
> ✓ written notice, or a demand in any form

**Pronouns.** Never use one that could point at more than one antecedent. Repeat the noun.

**Connectors.** Decide whether the test is conjunctive or disjunctive and write it. Never `and/or`.

---

## Step 7 — Housekeeping

- **Designation and heading** for each provision.
- **Effective date and transition.** Say what happens to matters pending on the effective date.
- **Severability.** State whether the provisions are severable.
- **Same word, same thing, every time.** Varied vocabulary is evidence of varied meaning.
- **Numerals once:** `14 days`, never `fourteen (14) days`.
- **Every period** states length, trigger, and direction: `within 21 days after service`.

---

## Step 8 — Scan

Read `prohibited.md` and check your draft against it.

Then confirm the drafting-specific points:

1. Every operative sentence carries exactly one of the seven operators.
2. No `shall` and no `should`, anywhere, at any register.
3. Every duty names its bearer, and the bearer is a person, not a document.
4. Every condition appears before the duty it governs.
5. Every requirement you intend to enforce states its consequence and severity.
6. No modifier whose reach is unclear.
7. Singular throughout, except where a group genuinely acts together.
8. Every sentence within 30 words.

---

## When drafting turns into restating

If you find yourself reproducing language from an existing instrument — a model act, a parallel rule, a prior version — you have stopped drafting. The moment text comes from a source, `restate.md` governs: preserve its operators, its quantifiers, and its ambiguity, and do not improve it in passing.
