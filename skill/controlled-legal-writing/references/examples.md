# Fidelity failures

## 1. Supplying an actor

The impulse is to write actively, because active voice reads better. Where the source is agentless, active voice invents a duty-bearer.

> Source: "The record must be transmitted within 30 days."
> ✗ The clerk must transmit the record within 30 days.
> ✓ The record must be transmitted within 30 days. The rule does not state who must transmit it.

Do not narrow a class either.

> Source: "A party may object."
> ✗ The defendant may object.

Do not generalize one actor to another.

> Source: "The clerk must enter the judgment."
> ✗ The court must enter the judgment.
> *The clerk and the court are different actors with different powers.*

**Exception.** Where another provision supplies the actor, you may name it — with a citation to the provision that supplies it.

---

## 2. Resolving an ambiguity

This runs against your strongest habit. Completing an unclear provision feels helpful. It substitutes your reading for the law.

**Syntactic — a modifier whose reach is unclear:**

> Source: "a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent licensed in this state"
> ✗ The statute applies to a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent who is licensed in this state.
> ✓ The statute applies to "a lawyer, an accountant, or an agent licensed in this state." It is not clear whether the licensing requirement applies to all three or only to an agent.

**Semantic — a word with more than one meaning:**

> Source: "A party must give notice."
> ✗ A party must give written notice.
> ✓ The rule requires notice. It does not state whether notice must be written.

**Contested construction — courts have divided:**

> ✓ The statute uses "shall." The Sixth Circuit has construed this word as mandatory in this context. Refer to [citation]. The Ninth Circuit has construed it as directory. Refer to [citation].

**What you may never do:**

- Choose the better reading and present it as the rule.
- Choose the more common reading.
- Choose the reading that reads more smoothly.
- Silently omit the ambiguous provision.

---

## 3. Hedging over a gap

A hedge disguises an unstated ambiguity as a tendency. It is worse than silence, because it reads like knowledge.

> ✗ Notice generally must be in writing.
> ✓ The rule requires notice. It does not state whether notice must be written.

Banned in this role: *generally, typically, usually, in most cases, often.*

Use a consistent marking form: *The rule does not state…*, *The statute does not define…*, *Courts have divided on…*

---

## 4. Paraphrasing a standard

The central prohibition. No exception at any register.

> Source: "…for good cause…"
> ✗ …if it has a good reason.
> ✓ …for good cause.

> ✗ The judge's decision stands unless it was really unreasonable.
> ✓ The appellate court reviews the decision for abuse of discretion.

> ✗ The agency's rule is upheld if it makes sense.
> ✓ The court sets aside the rule only if it is arbitrary and capricious.

Replacing *clear and convincing evidence* with *strong evidence* drops the standard, even though it looks like a paraphrase.

At R4 a description may **follow** the standard, never replace it:

> ✓ R4: The court may suspend the rule for *good cause*. "Good cause" is a legal standard. Courts decide case by case what counts, so this summary cannot tell you whether a particular reason qualifies.

---

## 5. Inverting a negative

The most common fidelity failure in the standard. Both forms look like negation; they are opposites.

> Source: "A party is not required to attach the exhibits to the motion."
> ✗ A party must not attach the exhibits to the motion.
> ✓ A party need not attach the exhibits to the motion.

The wrong version converts an option into a prohibition.

| Form | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| must not | forbidden to act | not required to act |
| need not | not required to act | forbidden to act |
| may not | has no authority to act | might not act |

---

## 6. Strengthening or weakening an operator

> Source: "The court may, in its discretion, award costs."
> ✗ The court awards costs to the prevailing party.
> ✗ The court will award costs.
> ✓ The court may award costs. The decision is within the court's discretion.

> Source: "The clerk shall enter the default."
> ✗ The clerk may enter the default.

---

## 7. Dropping a quantifier

Small words, full legal weight, easy to lose.

| Source | Do not write |
|---|---|
| each party | the parties, all parties |
| any party | a party, every party |
| all of the following | the following |
| one or more of the following | the following |
| at least 14 days | 14 days |
| no more than 30 days | 30 days, about 30 days |

---

## 8. Flipping a connector

*And* versus *or* is the difference between a conjunctive and a disjunctive test — two different rules.

> Source: "…if the movant shows good cause and the opposing party is not prejudiced…"
> ✗ …if the movant shows good cause or the opposing party is not prejudiced…

---

## 9. Dropping *only*

Dropping an exclusivity word converts a limitation into a grant.

> Source: "The court may act only on motion."
> ✗ The court may act on motion.

Same for *solely* and *exclusively*.

---

## 10. Adding a consequence

Where the source states no consequence, the restatement states none — and says so.

> Source: "A party must file a statement of related cases with its opening brief."
> ✗ A party must file a statement of related cases with its opening brief, or the brief will be rejected.
> ✓ A party must file a statement of related cases with its opening brief. The rule does not state what happens if a party does not file it.

---

## 11. Adding a purpose, limit, or form

**Purpose** — adding one narrows the power.

> Source: "The court may seal the record."
> ✗ The court may seal the record to protect the privacy of the parties.
> ✓ The court may seal the record. The rule does not state on what grounds.

**Limit** — *reasonable* is a standard; the rule imposed none.

> Source: "The court may grant an extension."
> ✗ The court may grant a reasonable extension.
> ✓ The court may grant an extension. The rule does not limit the length.

**Form:**

> Source: "A party must give notice."
> ✗ A party must give written notice.

**Inference:**

> Source: "The court must hold a hearing within 14 days."
> ✗ The court must hold a hearing within 14 days, so a party should prepare quickly.

---

## 12. Characterizing

A closing synthesis is construction wearing the clothes of a summary.

> ✗ This rule gives the court unusually broad discretion.
> ✓ The rule states no standard for the court's decision.

Also banned: an opening that announces the text, an "It's not just X, it's Y" construction, and evaluative adjectives — *important, critical, key, crucial, significant.*

---

## 13. Substituting a synonym

Use the term the source uses, even where another term of art means the same thing in general usage.

> Source: "…the district court shall remand the case…"
> ✗ The district court must send the case back.
> ✓ The district court must remand the case.

*Petitioner* and *plaintiff* are not interchangeable. *Vacate* and *set aside* are not interchangeable in every context.

---

## 14. Converting prose to a list

The most consequential machine-prose failure. Four bullets tell the reader there are exactly four things. The source may not say that.

Convert only where the source's content is genuinely enumerated. Where it flows, let it flow.

---

## 15. Advising, predicting, applying

> ✗ You should file early, because clerks sometimes reject filings.
> ✓ The rule sets a deadline of 14 days.

> ✗ A court will usually grant an extension in these circumstances.
> ✓ The court may grant an extension for good cause.

> ✗ If your motion was filed on day 15, it was untimely.
> ✓ A party must file the motion within 14 days after service.

The line is between describing the source and using it. Using it is the practice of law.

# Style failures

## 16. Archaic pointing words

> ✗ Said motion shall be filed within the time period specified hereinabove.
> ✓ The party must file the motion within the period stated in section 4(a).

> ✗ The party of the first part hereby agrees to indemnify the party of the second part against any and all claims arising hereunder.
> ✓ The seller must indemnify the buyer against any claim that arises under this agreement.

If you cannot identify what the compound points at, the source was ambiguous — flag it, do not guess.

---

## 17. Nominalizations

> ✗ The petitioner must make application for an extension.
> ✓ The petitioner must apply for an extension.

> ✗ The court shall conduct a review of the record and make a determination.
> ✓ The court must review the record and decide.

> ✗ Service of the summons is a requirement before entry of a default.
> ✓ A party must serve the summons before the clerk enters a default.

**Watch the exception.** *Service, joinder, removal, remand, discovery, waiver* are terms of art. Keep them.

---

## 18. Phrasal verbs

> ✗ The requirements set forth in subsection (b) shall be carried out by the applicant.
> ✓ The applicant must meet the requirements in subsection (b).

**Exception:** *hand down* (an opinion), *set aside* (a judgment), *strike down* (a statute) are terms of art.

---

## 19. Latin that is merely Latin

> ✗ The statute governs all filings, motions, notices, etc.
> ✓ The statute governs filings, motions, and notices.

A Latin **term of art** is preserved and glossed by register:

> R2: The court acted *sua sponte*.
> R3: The court acted *sua sponte* — on its own, without a request from either party.
> R4: The court acted on its own. Neither party asked it to. Lawyers call this acting *sua sponte*.

---

## 20. Doublets

> ✗ The order is null and void and of no force or effect.
> ✓ The order has no effect.

> ✗ Each and every party must sign.
> ✓ Each party must sign.

**Two exceptions:** settled terms of art (*aid and abet*, *pain and suffering*), and any doublet the source used — a court may have given each word separate work.

---

## 21. Intensifiers

> ✗ The statute clearly does not apply to this situation.
> ✓ The statute applies only to filings made after January 1, 2026. This filing was made in 2024.

> ✗ The appellate court will reverse only if the finding was very wrong.
> ✓ The appellate court will reverse only if the finding was clearly erroneous.

The second pair is the trap: *clearly erroneous* is a standard, not an intensifier.

---

## 22. and/or

> ✗ The court may impose a fine and/or costs.
> ✓ The court may impose a fine, costs, or both.

Where the **source** uses it, preserve it and flag it. Do not resolve it.

---

## 23. Semicolons

> ✗ The clerk must enter the judgment; the parties must then file a bill of costs.
> ✓ The clerk must enter the judgment. Each party must then file a bill of costs.

**Exceptions:** quoted text, and a restatement reproducing a source's enumerated series where the semicolon carries the *; and* / *; or* connector.

---

## 24. Serial comma

> ✗ distribution, packing for shipment and storage
> ✓ distribution, packing for shipment, and storage
> ✓ distribution, and packing for shipment and storage

The two correct forms mean different things. The incorrect one means neither. A wage case has turned on this.

---

## 25. Numbers twice

> ✗ within a period of not more than fourteen (14) days from and after the date of service
> ✓ within 14 days after service

Every period needs length, trigger, and direction.

---

## 26. Varied vocabulary

Your training says vary the words. In law, varied vocabulary is evidence of varied meaning.

> ✗ The movant must file the motion. The petition must state the grounds for relief. The application must be served on each party.
> ✓ The movant must file the motion. The motion must state the grounds for relief. The movant must serve the motion on each party.

The repetition is correct. Never use a pronoun that could point at more than one antecedent.

> ✗ The clerk must file the notice with the court and serve it on the parties. It must be dated.
> ✓ The clerk must file the notice with the court and serve the notice on each party. The notice must be dated.
